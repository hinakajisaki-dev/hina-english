## 3. Notes

### Before you film (do these first)
1. **Get permission in writing** from 株式会社二期 (NIKI) to film and publish (広告掲載承諾). It's the same seller as the Tokiwamatsu unit, so you can ask for both at once.
2. **Ask building management (Nomura Real Estate Partners)** before filming the lobby, the entrance hall or the delivery lockers.
3. **Check the price and availability** on filming day and again on upload day.
4. **Update the exchange rate** on upload day, and put the ¥ and $ amounts with the rate and date in the description.
5. **Don't show:** the agent's personal phone number or email, residents, mailbox or locker name tags, other units' doors, car license plates, or school children's faces.
6. **Don't call it "新築" (new build) in Japanese on-screen text or captions.** Under Japan's real estate advertising rules, "新築" only applies within one year of completion. This building was completed in July 2025, so it's past that now. Say **"never lived in"** or **"unused"** (未入居) instead. It's still true, and it keeps you accurate.
7. **No drone.** Central Tokyo needs government permission for drone flights.

### ⚠️ Confirm with the agent before saying on camera
- [ ] **Is the price tax-included?** The first flyer said 税込 and this one doesn't.
- [ ] **What is the "管理者業務委託費" (¥1,727/month)?** If the building uses a **third-party manager system** (第三者管理者方式), owners may not have to serve on the owners' board. That's a big plus for overseas owners, because in most Japanese condos owners take turns on the board. If it's confirmed, add a LOCAL TIP about it. If not, skip it.
- [ ] **What does the "smart condo service" (¥1,991/month) include?** (An app? Internet? Energy monitoring?)
- [ ] Who owns the **7 homes that weren't sold to the public**? (Landowner units? Rentals?) This affects the mix of owners and renters.
- [ ] The **repair reserve plan**: new buildings often start low and step up over time. Ask for the long-term repair plan and the increase schedule (長期修繕計画).
- [ ] The warranty: the seller's period (契約不適合責任), and whether **Nomura's after-service warranty** can be transferred to the new owner.
- [ ] **Which direction** the curved window faces and what the view is (and whether anything is planned next door that could block it). This matters more because the zoning is mixed-use.
- [ ] The toilet brand and features; whether the bath has auto-fill and reheat (オートバス・追い焚き).
- [ ] Whether the entrance hall really has the round shape with mirror and bronze panels (this comes from other online listings).
- [ ] Is 24-hour security staffed? The flyer says "全部委託（日勤）", meaning a day-shift manager. Some online listings mention 24-hour security, so ask how it works.
- [ ] Pet rules (number and size limits).
- [ ] Parking and bike space availability.
- [ ] **Annual property tax** (固定資産税・都市計画税).
- [ ] Whether this is **the largest layout in the building**. One listing site says units range from 31 to 71.62 m². If true, it's a good talking point.
- [ ] Balcony rules (laundry, plants, storage).
- [ ] Walking times to Life, Shibuya Stream and the river walkway, Daikanyama and Ebisu. Walk them yourself.
