## 3. Notes

### Before you film (do these first)
1. **Get permission in writing.** The flyer asks you to contact the seller for 広告掲載承諾 (permission to advertise). Ask 株式会社二期 (NIKI) for permission to film **and** publish, and ask whether you can show the price, the unit number and the floor plan.
2. **Ask building management (Tokyu Community or the concierge)** before filming the lobby, lounge or any common area.
3. **Check the price and availability** on filming day and again on upload day.
4. **Update the exchange rate** on upload day. Show both ¥ and $, with the rate and date in the description.
5. **Don't show:** the agent's personal phone number or email from the flyer, residents, other units' doors or numbers, car license plates, children's faces, or the police boxes and security around the imperial residence. Film the forest from the unit or a public street; don't zoom into the grounds.
6. **No drone.** Central Tokyo is a densely populated area that needs government permission for drones, and airspace around imperial facilities can be a no-fly zone.

### Foreign-buyer notes (off camera, or for a future video)
- Foreigners, including non-residents, can generally buy property in Japan.
- Non-residents usually have to file a report after buying under the Foreign Exchange Act, and they need a tax representative (納税管理人) to pay property tax. Rules on foreign ownership were being reviewed in 2025–26, so **check the current rules before you give specifics on camera**.
- Japanese mortgages are hard to get for non-residents, and many overseas buyers pay cash. Don't promise financing.
- Never predict price growth or promise rental yields. Present numbers as facts, not forecasts.

### Keeping it credible
- **Say what you don't know.** "I'll confirm that and put it in the comments" builds more trust than a guess.
- **Every episode, show the same numbers card:** price (¥/$), m²/sq ft, ¥ per m², year built, monthly fees, station walking times. Viewers learn to compare, and they come back for it.
- **Avoid superlatives** like "best," "cheapest," "guaranteed" or "a steal." Let the facts carry the video.
- **Your advantage is being a local.** Other channels show the rooms. You can explain *why* Japanese homes work the way they do. Lean into the LOCAL TIP segments.
- **If you're not a licensed real estate agent**, don't present yourself as one or take inquiries as a broker. Send buyers to the listing company or to a licensed partner, and say so in the description.
- **Tone:** talk like you're showing a friend from abroad around your neighborhood. Warm, and then precise when you get to the numbers.
